# Figure style

The conventions the figures in both notebooks follow. They are set in
`src/coip/figures.py`; this file is the reference for why.

Figures should look like those in a well-designed biology paper: restrained,
readable, no decoration. One figure, one result. The simplest plot that shows the
data accurately, readable at the size it will be printed.


## Layout and typography

White background, thin black axes at about 0.75 pt, left and bottom spines only
where that suits the plot, outward ticks, no gridlines.

No titles inside the plotting area and no explanatory text on the canvas; the
caption in the notebook carries the explanation.

Multipanel figures get bold panel letters A, B, C just outside the top-left of each
panel, added by `figures.save`. Axes are aligned and scales shared where panels are
meant to be compared.

Arial, Helvetica or Liberation Sans. 8 pt base, 8.5 pt axis labels, 7.5 pt ticks and
legends, 9 pt bold panel letters, all set by `figures.use_style()`.

Axis labels name the quantity and its transformation, for example `log₂ intensity`
or `log₂ fold change (RAPA/DMSO)`.


## Colour

Okabe-Ito is the default categorical palette, because it is colour-blind safe:

| Colour | Hex |
|---|---|
| Black | `#000000` |
| Orange | `#E69F00` |
| Sky blue | `#56B4E9` |
| Bluish green | `#009E73` |
| Yellow | `#F0E442` |
| Blue | `#0072B2` |
| Vermillion | `#D55E00` |
| Reddish purple | `#CC79A7` |

Colour answers one of two questions, and never both in one figure.

**Which condition a run belongs to.**

| Role | Colour | FR002 | CK001 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Negative control | Bluish green `#009E73` | the control | NF54 |
| Bait pull-down, untreated | Blue `#0072B2` | the PfSUN1 pull-down | DMSO |
| Bait pull-down, treated | Orange `#E69F00` | — | RAPA |

`figures.CONTROL_COLOUR` and `PULLDOWN_COLOUR`. CK001 sets its treated colour in its
own configuration cell.

**What a point or bar segment stands for.**

| Role | Colour | Constant |
|---|---|---|
| The bait | Black `#000000` | `BAIT_COLOUR` |
| Proteins the figure is about | Orange `#E69F00` | `HIGHLIGHT_COLOUR` |
| Background | Light grey `#D9D9D9` | `BACKGROUND_GREY` |
| Background subdivisions | Grey ramp | `BACKGROUND_RAMP` |

Grey for context, colour for the result.

### Experiment-specific palettes

Approved exceptions, set in that notebook's configuration cell rather than in
`figures.py`:

- **FR002 protein classes** (`composition`, `top_contributors`). *P. falciparum* in
  Okabe-Ito blue `#0072B2`; human classes as a pink ramp off reddish purple:
  `#8E416C`, `#B76294`, `#CC79A7`, `#E9C3D8`; mixed species `#444444`; unassigned
  `#EEEEEE`. Approved 13 November 2026.
- **CK001 conditions and selected proteins.** RAPA in orange `#E69F00` rather than
  vermillion, and the proteins a figure is about in sky blue `#56B4E9` rather than
  orange, so the two never collide in one figure (`SELECTED_COLOUR`). Approved
  17 November 2026.

### Matrices

A matrix needs a perceptually uniform sequential map: `figures.SIMILARITY_MAP`, a
single blue hue, or viridis where the full range has to be read at a glance, which
is what both run-agreement heatmaps use. Avoid rainbow maps, and use a diverging map
only when the quantity has a real midpoint.

Draw the full symmetric matrix when reading along a row matters, and leave the
diagonal blank: a run compared with itself is not a measurement.

### Accessibility

Figures stay interpretable in greyscale and for readers with colour-vision
deficiency. Where colour alone would carry the message, position, shape or an
outline carries it too. Fills are the exact hex at full opacity; transparency is for
dense scatter overplotting only, and the caption says so.


## Labels and legends

Axis and tick labels are black. Prefer a direct label on the data over a legend, and
keep legends short and outside the data region unless inside is genuinely better.

Label a handful of proteins, not every outlier. `figures.label_points` places them,
trying short offsets first and drawing a leader only where a name had to move.


## Statistics and missing values

- Show individual observations when the sample size is small; prefer dot plots to
  bars for few replicates.
- Never invent an error bar or a confidence interval.
- Distinguish technical from biological replication, and never let a figure imply
  biological replication the design does not have.
- Draw a significance threshold only where the design supports a test and something
  reaches it. A fold-change guide is a magnitude, and the caption says so.
- A missing measurement is never drawn as a zero and never imputed. Show it as a gap
  or an open marker, count it, and say in the caption which is which.


## Per plot type

- **Composition.** Stacked bars, stable category order, one legend outside the panels.
- **Measurement depth.** Separate panels for quantities on different scales. Where
  one count is a subset of another, stack them so an equal pair shows no gap.
- **Run agreement.** Every pair scored on the same protein groups, named in the
  caption.
- **Normalisation.** Before and after on one shared scale, with the offsets shown.
- **Protein recovery.** Every individual measurement, one lane per condition. A
  protein not quantified in a run is an open ring in a separate strip.
- **Volcano.** log₂ fold change against the adjusted p-value, both named on the axes,
  tested and significant counts in a corner, identical axis limits across panels.
- **SAINTexpress.** Observed counts and model-derived scores in separate panels,
  never one composite score. The bait is set apart in the bait colour.


## Export

`figures.use_style()` once at the top of a notebook. Widths come from
`figures.SINGLE_COLUMN` (85 mm) and `figures.DOUBLE_COLUMN` (180 mm); height follows
the content. `figures.save(fig, name, directory, panel_axes=axes)` adds the panel
letters and writes PDF, SVG and 400 dpi PNG together, with text kept editable
(`pdf.fonttype 42`, `svg.fonttype none`).
